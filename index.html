How it works

    
Adding a tip

    
Your story is read by Gemini Nano, the small model built into Chrome, on your own device. That step is set to on-device only, so if Nano isn’t available it will not quietly send your text to the cloud. Fixed rules remove names, phone numbers, emails and roll numbers before and after the model. You edit and approve a short tip, and only that tip is stored, with no account id.

    
Asking

    
Your question and the closest tips go to Gemini through Firebase AI Logic, preferring the cloud. If you’re offline, the same question runs on your device instead, and the answer says which one it was. Every claim has to point at a source or it is dropped. “Heard from several students” needs at least three tips, and a lone story is always labelled as one.

    
What’s real

    
Halden University is fictional. Handbook entries and tips marked “Sample” are invented for the demo. Tips added through this site appear as “Community tip, unreviewed”. Anonymous sign-in lets you delete your own tips later. The tip itself doesn’t carry your account id. A separate private record links the two, and only that same anonymous account can read or delete it.
